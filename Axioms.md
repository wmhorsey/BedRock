## Abstract

The substrate is described by a single self-interacting scalar field $A(\mathbf{x}, t)$. Its local attraction density is defined by $T = \frac{1}{\alpha}\nabla^2 A$, and all structure and motion emerge from the Aptik Geodesic Principle of maximal density conservation together with strict local energy conservation. There is no drag, no inertial momentum, and no resistance.

## Axioms

0. **Local Energy Conservation of the Substrate**

   The substrate cannot create or destroy energy. All changes in tension require energy transfer from adjacent substrate. Gradients are the only mechanism for work. The void core is the only region with zero energy availability.

1. **Substrate field**

   The substrate is a single self-interacting scalar field $A(\mathbf{x}, t)$.

2. **Attraction density and field**

   The local attraction density is given by

   $$
   T(\mathbf{x}, t) = \frac{1}{\alpha} \nabla^2 A(\mathbf{x}, t)
   $$

   and the attraction field is

   $$
   \mathbf{G} = \nabla A.
   $$

   Flows and test particles move toward regions of higher $A$ in the direction of $\mathbf{G}$.

3. **Core–ambient continuum**

   Every core is defined by $\Delta A = A_{\text{amb}} - A_{\text{core}}$ and lies on a single continuum: void if $\Delta A > 0$, depression if $\Delta A \approx 0$ (relative to the local ambient scale), spike if $\Delta A < 0$.

4. **Universal structure of entities**

   Every entity has structure $((((\text{Core})\ \text{Shell})\ \text{Taper})\ \text{Ambient})$, where the shell is the surface of maximum $|\nabla A|$, and the taper is the smooth fall-off to ambient.

5. **Shell bias and motion**

   Shell bias is $\Delta A_{\text{shell}} = A_{\text{out}} - A_{\text{in}}$, with outward, neutral, or inward bias for $\Delta A_{\text{shell}} > 0$, $\approx 0$, $< 0$ respectively. Motion arises from shell–ambient mismatch.

6. **Composite cores and discs**

   Multiple void cores confined together force excess substrate into a self-attracting accretion disc governed by the same Poisson relation. Proton: $(u_{\text{void}}, u_{\text{void}}, d_{\text{spike}})$. Neutron: $(u_{\text{void}}, d_{\text{spike}}, d_{\text{spike}})$. Maximum of two voids per stable baryon.

7. **Scale breaking by voids**

   Only void cores break scale, with $A_{\text{core}} = 0$. All other structures scale with ambient.

8. **Bound Substrate Restriction (Mass Definition)**

   Mass is the total bound substrate restriction: the integrated attraction density $T = \frac{1}{\alpha}\nabla^2 A$ that is geometrically locked inside the $((((\text{Core})\ \text{Shell})\ \text{Taper}))$ structure — including all wake lobes, moving curvature, and local field presence — before it can escape as free flow or tension-release quasi-particles.

   This restriction increases whenever $|\nabla A|$ steepens or shell/wake curvature tightens (Axioms 0, 9, 12). Consequently, an electromagnet acquires a small additional mass while current flows (macro-entangled wake curvature, derived law 2.4) and returns to its baseline mass when the current ceases.

9. **Wakes and bonding**

   Electron "clouds" are low-attraction wake zones $\Delta A_{\text{wake}} = A_{\text{shared}} - A_{\text{ambient}} \ll 0$.

10. **Field compression instead of time dilation**

    Higher tension or steeper $|\nabla A|$ increases a local compression factor $C(\mathbf{x})$, so $v_{\text{eff}} = v_0 / C(\mathbf{x})$.

11. **Aptik Geodesic Principle**

    A path $\gamma$ is geodesic if

    $$
    \delta \int_{\gamma} T(\mathbf{x})\, ds = 0.
    $$

12. **Dynamic Shell and Tension Release**

    At the shell surface (maximum $|\nabla A|$), high-tension regions drive inward flows while trapped low-tension regions ($\Delta A > 0$) are released outward as low-tension quasi-particles. Energy is transferred locally via gradients (Axiom 0).

13. **Curvature-Driven Horizon Dynamics**

    Tighter outer curvature produces stronger local gradients and more frequent/intense quasi-particle release (energy conserved via Axiom 0).

14. **Depression Cores as Substrate Condensates**

    A depression core ($\Delta A \approx 0$) is a metastable, coherent pocket of trapped substrate energy.

15. **Hierarchical Shell Structure and Ambient Inheritance**

    Hierarchy emerges automatically from the geometry of $A(\mathbf{x}, t)$ and its gradients. Every stable structure possesses the same four-part organization $((((\text{Core})\ \text{Shell})\ \text{Taper})\ \text{Ambient})$.
